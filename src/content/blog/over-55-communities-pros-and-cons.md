Have you considered the pros and cons of living in an age 55 and over community?

Many adults 55 and older think about their next living situation. One popular option is moving into an over-55 community, also called an **active adult community**. These communities are designed for people age fifty-five and older and offer benefits tailored to empty nesters and retirees. As with any lifestyle choice, there are pros and cons to weigh before diving in. Let's look at both to help you decide whether this lifestyle fits your preferences and goals.

## What is an over-55 community?

Over-55 communities are residential developments or neighborhoods built specifically for older adults, with a minimum age requirement. Usually at least one resident in each household must be 55 or older. The focus is on a lifestyle centered on leisure, convenience and community.

Amenities vary from one community to the next. Common features include single-story homes or condominiums designed for easy accessibility, golf courses, fitness centers, pools and clubhouses. Many offer recreational and social activities and maintenance-free living, where landscaping, lawn care and snow removal are handled for you.

## The pros of living in an over-55 community

**A sense of community.** You can connect with like-minded neighbors at a similar stage of life. Many communities are set up to encourage friendships through social events and clubs such as book clubs, card tournaments, yoga classes and group outings. Building friendships and staying socially active supports emotional and mental well-being.

**Low-maintenance living.** Little or no home maintenance means more time for hobbies and travel and less time on chores.

**Age-friendly features.** Homes may include wider doorways and bathroom grab bars that help prevent falls. Walking trails, aerobics, swimming, nutrition classes, meditation and health seminars help residents stay active and engaged.

**Peace of mind and security.** Some communities have gated entrances, security patrols or neighborhood watch programs. Being surrounded by neighbors also creates a built-in support system that helps in emergencies.

**A simpler home.** Smaller homes are easier to manage and can mean lower utility bills. Downsizing and letting go of things you no longer need can feel liberating.

**New connections for empty nesters.** If your children are grown or you live alone, a 55+ community is a chance to meet others and focus on your own interests.

## The cons to consider

**Less variety in daily interactions.** With most neighbors in the same age range, some people feel they are missing the energy of a mixed-age neighborhood. If you spend a lot of time with younger family members who don't meet the age rules, it can feel isolating.

**Higher costs.** Homes can cost more than similar homes in traditional neighborhoods, and homeowners' association (HOA) fees add up and can increase over time.

**Rules and restrictions.** HOA rules may limit home modifications or the number and length of guest visits. Residents used to full independence may find this stifling, and the limited range of home styles may not suit you if you prefer a two-story or larger home.

**Harder to sell.** Because buyers must meet the age requirement, the pool of potential buyers is smaller when it's time to sell.

**Location.** Many communities are in suburban or rural areas, which can mean longer trips to healthcare and shopping. If you don't drive, public transportation may be limited.

**Social pressure.** Some people feel pressure to join every activity. If you're more of a private person, the constant social calendar may feel awkward.

## Is an over-55 community right for you?

It comes down to your lifestyle and priorities. Ask yourself:

- Do I value a strong sense of community?
- Am I looking for low-maintenance, hassle-free homeownership?
- Would I prefer a mixed-age neighborhood?
- Have I reviewed my budget, and do the HOA fees fit comfortably?

If you're searching for the right community, take your time. Visit several. Tour the neighborhood and surrounding area. Ask about restrictions and every cost. If you can, talk with current residents about what they like and don't like. And think about the future: choose a place that will continue to fit you as you age.

Living in a 55+ community can be rewarding, and for many people the social connections outweigh the drawbacks. Make sure it matches your expectations, and you may find the perfect place to enjoy your retirement years in comfort.
