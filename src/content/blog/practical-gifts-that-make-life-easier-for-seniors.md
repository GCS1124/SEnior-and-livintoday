## Think about the person, not the age label

There is no one perfect gift for every older adult. Some people love technology; others prefer familiar, simple things. Before buying, think about the person's interests, routines, space, and preferences. A gift should feel personal—not like a reminder of what someone can no longer do.

## Choose gifts that support daily comfort

A comfortable reading light, easy-to-use kitchen item, warm blanket, large-print calendar, or helpful organizer may be welcome if it matches the person's needs. For mobility or safety equipment, ask the person and, when appropriate, a health professional before purchasing. A well-meant device that does not fit can become clutter.

## Give the gift of connection

Plan a lunch date, a regular phone call, a family photo book, or a class you can attend together. The National Institute on Aging highlights hobbies, classes, volunteering, and time with friends and family as meaningful ways to stay connected. Experiences can be more valuable than another object on a shelf.

## Support a hobby they already enjoy

Consider gardening supplies, art materials, a puzzle, a book subscription, a bird feeder, or ingredients for a favorite recipe. Keep the activity affordable and suited to the person's comfort and abilities. The best gift is one they will actually use and enjoy.

## Ask before you buy

A simple question can prevent a disappointing gift: "Would this make your day easier, or would you rather choose something else?" Respect the answer. The goal is to celebrate the person and make life a little more enjoyable.

## Quick takeaways

- Choose based on personal interests and routines.

- Prioritize comfort and usefulness over novelty.

- Consider shared experiences and regular time together.

- Ask before buying equipment or safety products.

Before buying a gift, ask yourself: Will this make the person feel known, included, or more comfortable?
