## A small monthly review can prevent last-minute scrambling

Families do not need a complicated system to stay organized. A short monthly check-in can help everyone understand what is coming up and whether an older loved one needs anything. It is also a chance to notice changes before they become urgent.

## Review health information and appointments

Check that the medication list is current and that upcoming appointments, transportation, and prescription refills are planned. Do not change treatment or medication without guidance from the appropriate clinician. Write down questions the older adult wants to ask at the next visit.

## Check the home and everyday routines

Ask whether any part of the home feels harder to use. Look at lighting, walkways, stairs, bathroom safety, and seasonal needs. Check that smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are working according to manufacturer guidance. If there has been a fall or a change in mobility, discuss it with a health professional.

## Confirm contacts and emergency plans

Make sure family members know whom to call, where essential documents are stored, and what the plan is if transportation, power, or regular care becomes unavailable. Ready.gov recommends that older adults plan for medications, medical supplies, communication, transportation, and assistive devices as part of emergency preparedness.

## Ask one important question: what has changed?

The older adult may be doing well and need no new support. Or a small task may have become difficult. Ask what would make the next month easier. Update the plan together and assign any agreed tasks to specific people. A short, respectful conversation can keep the family on the same page.

## Quick takeaways

- Review appointments, medication information, and transportation.

- Check home safety and seasonal needs.

- Confirm emergency contacts and document locations.

- Ask what has changed and agree on one next step.

Put a 20-minute family check-in on the calendar for the first week of next month.
