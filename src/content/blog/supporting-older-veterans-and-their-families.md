## A lifetime of service deserves thoughtful support

For many older veterans, military service is one chapter in a much larger life story. They may be parents, grandparents, neighbors, volunteers, and people who have spent decades looking after others. As they grow older, they may need help with appointments, daily tasks, benefits paperwork, or simply staying connected. Offering support should never mean taking away their voice. The goal is to make it easier for a veteran to live safely, comfortably, and with dignity.

## Start with a conversation, not assumptions

Ask what would make everyday life easier. Does getting to appointments take more effort? Are letters from the Department of Veterans Affairs piling up? Would help with home maintenance or technology be useful? Listen before jumping into solutions. Some veterans may welcome hands-on help; others may prefer that a family member simply sit beside them while they make calls or review information.

## Know where to look for support

The U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs offers several resources for eligible veterans and caregivers. Depending on the veteran's circumstances, support may include health care, disability-related benefits, home adaptations, caregiver education, counseling, peer support, or respite services. Eligibility varies, so families should use official VA information and speak with a VA representative rather than assume a benefit applies. The VA Caregiver Support Program can help caregivers understand available services and connect with a local support team.

## Make practical help feel respectful

Offer to organize appointment information, create a current medication list to review with the care team, arrange transportation, or help keep important documents together. Ask permission before contacting an agency or sharing personal information. If a veteran has difficulty hearing, seeing, remembering details, or managing daily activities, a health professional can help assess what kind of support may be appropriate.

## A simple way to show appreciation

Respect is often found in small things: asking before helping, remembering a service-related milestone, listening to a story more than once, or making sure a veteran is included in family plans. Support should recognize the whole person—not only their military service or their age. A little patience and a reliable plan can make a meaningful difference.

## Quick takeaways

- Ask the veteran what kind of help they actually want.

- Explore official VA programs and verify eligibility directly.

- Keep important contacts, appointment details, and documents organized.

- Protect the veteran's choices, privacy, and independence.
