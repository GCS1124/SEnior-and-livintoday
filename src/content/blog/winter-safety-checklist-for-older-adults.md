## Winter preparation is about more than warm coats

Cold weather can affect older adults differently. The National Institute on Aging notes that aging, certain health conditions, and some medications can make it harder to recognize or respond to becoming too cold. Preparing the home and planning ahead can reduce avoidable stress.

## Check the home before temperatures drop

Make sure heating equipment is working and that smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are in good condition. Look for drafts around doors and windows. Keep warm clothing, blankets, and easy-to-prepare food available. Follow manufacturer instructions for heaters and never use an oven or outdoor grill to heat a home. If heating costs are a concern, ask a local Area Agency on Aging or community organization about assistance programs.

## Plan for safe trips outdoors

Keep walkways clear of snow and ice, use handrails, and wear shoes with nonslip soles. Avoid unnecessary trips during icy conditions. Arrange grocery or prescription delivery when available, or ask a trusted neighbor or family member for help. If driving is necessary, keep a charged phone and appropriate emergency supplies in the vehicle.

## Keep essentials and contacts ready

Maintain an up-to-date medication list and know how to contact health care providers. Ask the clinician or pharmacist whether any medicines may affect temperature regulation or balance. If power or heat is lost, have a plan for where to go and who can check in. Ready.gov recommends that older adults and people with disabilities plan for medications, medical supplies, communication, transportation, and assistive devices.

## Make a check-in plan

Agree on who will call or visit during severe weather. A quick daily check-in can help identify a heating problem, a fall, or a need for supplies. If someone shows signs of hypothermia—such as confusion, slurred speech, or unusual drowsiness—seek emergency help. Call 911 for a suspected medical emergency.

## Quick takeaways

- Check heating, alarms, drafts, and warm supplies.

- Clear outdoor paths and plan alternatives for icy days.

- Keep medication and emergency contact information current.

- Arrange regular check-ins during severe weather.

Use this checklist before the next cold snap and share it with an older neighbor or family member.
