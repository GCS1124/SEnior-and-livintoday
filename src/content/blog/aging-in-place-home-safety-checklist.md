Most older adults say they would rather stay in their own homes as they age. With a few thoughtful changes, many can do so safely and comfortably for years. Use this room-by-room checklist on your own, with a family member or with a caregiver to spot easy fixes before they become problems.

## Throughout the home

- Clear walkways of clutter, cords and loose items.
- Remove or secure throw rugs with non-slip backing.
- Add bright, even lighting, plus night-lights in hallways and between the bedroom and bathroom.
- Put light switches within easy reach at room entrances, or use plug-in or smart lights.
- Test smoke and carbon monoxide detectors twice a year. Our [twice-a-year safety habit](/blog/check-smoke-detectors-change-furnace-filter/) makes it easy to remember.
- Keep a list of emergency contacts and medications on the refrigerator.

## Stairs and entryways

- Install sturdy handrails on both sides of every staircase.
- Make sure each step is well lit, with a switch at the top and the bottom.
- Mark step edges with contrasting tape if depth is hard to see.
- Keep outdoor steps and walkways clear of ice, leaves and debris.
- Consider a bench by the door for setting down bags and putting on shoes.

## Bathroom

- Install grab bars by the toilet and inside the tub or shower. Towel bars are not built to hold body weight.
- Use non-slip mats or strips inside and outside the tub.
- Add a shower chair and a handheld shower head.
- Consider a raised toilet seat if standing up is difficult.
- Set the water heater to a safe temperature to prevent scalds.

## Kitchen

- Store everyday items between waist and shoulder height to avoid ladders and bending.
- Use a sturdy step stool with a handle if you must reach high shelves, or ask for help.
- Keep a fire extinguisher nearby and know how to use it.
- Consider appliances with automatic shut-off features.

## Bedroom

- Keep a lamp, phone and flashlight within reach of the bed.
- Make sure the bed is a comfortable height to sit and stand.
- Arrange furniture so there is a clear path to the door and bathroom.

## Staying connected and prepared

- Keep a charged phone nearby at all times, and consider a medical alert device.
- Set up regular check-ins with family, friends or a [senior care virtual assistant](/services/senior-care-virtual-assistant/).
- Keep important information such as medications, doctors, insurance and emergency wishes in one place. [The Living Legacy Planner](/books/living-legacy-planner/) includes a Temporary Instructions section for caregivers who step in after an illness or surgery.
- Review your medications with your doctor or pharmacist, since some can cause dizziness.
