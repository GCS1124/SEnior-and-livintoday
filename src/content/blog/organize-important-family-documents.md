## An emergency is not the best time to search

Imagine needing a medication list, insurance card, doctor's phone number, or legal document in a hurry. Would you know where to look? Many families keep important information in different drawers, email accounts, and filing cabinets. Bringing the essentials together can make stressful moments easier to manage.

## Build a practical family information file

Start with basic identification and contact details, health care providers, allergies, current medications, insurance information, and emergency contacts. Add copies or location details for important legal and financial documents, such as advance directives, powers of attorney, wills, and relevant account records. The National Institute on Aging offers a worksheet that can help families identify important documents and paperwork.

## Keep sensitive information secure

Organization should never mean leaving private information exposed. Store originals in a secure place, such as a locked or fire-resistant box where appropriate. Keep copies or a simple index in a separate secure location. Do not place Social Security numbers, account passwords, or full financial details in an ordinary shared document or unsecured email. Use a reputable password manager or another secure method for access information, and make sure the trusted person knows how to reach it.

## Decide who should know what

Choose one or two trusted people who know where the information is stored. Make sure the older adult agrees to the arrangement and understands who can access it. Legal authority matters: being a family member does not automatically give someone permission to make medical or financial decisions. Documents and state laws determine who may act and when.

## Review the file once or twice a year

Update the medication list, insurance details, emergency contacts, and provider information whenever something changes. Remove outdated copies so family members do not rely on old instructions. A short review at the start of a new month or after an annual appointment can keep the file useful.

## Quick takeaways

- Create one organized index of essential information.

- Include health, legal, financial, and emergency contacts.

- Protect passwords and sensitive personal information.

- Confirm legal authority rather than assuming it.

Choose one small step today: gather the essential contacts and write down where the important documents are kept.
