## A hospital stay can be overwhelming

Hospitals move quickly. There may be new medications, unfamiliar terms, follow-up appointments, equipment, and instructions to remember. An older adult who is tired, uncomfortable, or recovering from an illness may find it difficult to take everything in. Having a trusted family member or caregiver involved—with the patient's permission—can help make sure important questions are asked and answers are written down.

## Be part of the discharge conversation

Discharge planning should not be treated as a last-minute conversation at the door. Ask the care team what needs to happen before the patient leaves. What symptoms should prompt a call? Which medications should continue, stop, or change? Is there a special diet, wound care, mobility support, or follow-up visit? Who should the family contact if something is unclear? Medicare provides a discharge planning checklist that patients and caregivers can use during a hospital or other care stay.

## Make sure the home is ready

Think about the first few days at home. Is there a safe path from the bedroom to the bathroom? Will the person need help with meals, bathing, dressing, stairs, or transportation? Are prescriptions available? Does someone understand how to use any equipment? If the family cannot provide the needed support, ask the discharge planner or social worker about appropriate services and options.

## Keep the information in one place

Create a folder with the discharge summary, updated medication list, appointment dates, care instructions, provider phone numbers, and questions for the next visit. Share relevant information with the patient's clinicians. Do not change medication doses or treatment plans based on guesswork; contact the prescribing professional when instructions conflict or are unclear.

## The patient should remain at the center

Family involvement can be valuable, but the older adult should remain part of decisions whenever possible. Ask what they understand, what worries them, and what support they are comfortable accepting. A calm, prepared support person can help turn a confusing transition into a more manageable plan.

## Quick takeaways

- Ask questions before discharge, not after arriving home.

- Use Medicare's discharge planning checklist.

- Confirm medication instructions with the care team.

- Plan transportation, meals, mobility, and follow-up care.

Save this article and share it with anyone who may be helping an older loved one prepare to leave the hospital.
